# Conception

Resolve the design needed for the present destination, not every decision a future automated system might face. Deliver a bounded, evidence-led conclusion that preparation can consume without guessing which ideas were accepted.

## Establish the Destination

Inspect the actual project, existing decisions and supplied evidence before proposing an approach. Use requirements and relevant surrounding conventions when no implementation exists; do not invent code to satisfy discovery. State the destination, exclusions and observable success criteria. Mark a criterion as provisional rather than choosing the user's priority for them.

Keep verified facts separate from interpretations, recommendations and explicitly accepted decisions. Attach a source and its limits to each decision-critical empirical claim. Treat user answers as preferences or approvals, not technical proof.

## Resolve What Blocks This Decision

Route technical questions to code, tests, runtime observations or authoritative sources; route intent and trade-offs to the user. Ask one material question at a time with meaningful options through an available interactive tool. If none is available, present the same choices in text. Avoid asking what inspection can establish.

Compare genuinely different approaches using fit to the destination, complexity, reversibility and failure conditions. Include a direct baseline and a materially different alternative; consider a structural or simplifying alternative where useful. Verify empirical assumptions before recommending. Do not assign invented probabilities to options.

Keep future contingencies visible with their trigger rather than solving them prematurely. Propose a minimal reversible experiment when dialogue and existing evidence cannot settle an experiential or technical uncertainty; obtain the authority needed for any side effects.

## Converge and Stop

Conclude when the destination of this discussion is achieved and no unresolved choice blocks the next preparation step. Record:

- Destination, constraints and exclusions.
- Verified evidence and its limits.
- Accepted decisions and reasons, separately from unaccepted proposals.
- Rejected alternatives and why they were rejected.
- Remaining questions, their impact, whether they block progress and how or when to resolve them.
- Observable acceptance criteria and the next permitted step.

Do not revive a rejected path without new evidence, changed intent or an explicit user decision. Do not extend a manual workflow design into a factory, scheduler or retry-policy project just to fill a template.

Reuse an existing sufficient conclusion. Preserve its unresolved choices rather than polishing them into certainty. Transfer the result to preparation when requested; do not launch implementation or commit documents merely because conception ended.
